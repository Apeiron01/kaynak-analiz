How to Fix an App Store 4.3 Spam Rejection (4.3a / 4.3b): A Step-by-Step Path

A "Guideline 4.3 - Design - Spam" rejection is confusing, especially on a first submission. This guide combines Apple's rule text with what we learned taking our own game, FluxCore, through a 4.3(a) review.

11 November 2026App StoreApp ReviewiOS

Quick Answer

A 4.3 rejection means Apple sees your app as a copy of something already on the store, or as a needless variant of the same app. Before sending a new build, reply to App Review and ask which app you were matched with. Then remove files your bundle may share byte-for-byte with other apps (stock music, open-source icon sets, template code), make what is genuinely different about your app visible, and resubmit with a note that lists the changes.

What does Guideline 4.3 actually say?

Apple's App Review Guidelines split 4.3 in two. 4.3(a) forbids submitting multiple Bundle IDs of the same app, with a separate map app for every city as the example. 4.3(b) rejects apps that are indistinguishable from what is already widely available, and names saturated categories such as flashlight, wallpaper, simple timer, fortune telling and dating apps.

In practice a 4.3(a) rejection says "this app looks too much like another app" — in code, in the binary, in the artwork or in the store text. 4.3(b) says "you are not offering anything new in this category".

Source: App Review Guidelines 4.3 Spam.

Why do apps get a 4.3 rejection?

Templates, ready-made game kits and reskins are the most common reason: hundreds of people ship the same template.

A less obvious one is files that are byte-identical to files in other apps. FluxCore bundled two very widely redistributed Creative Commons music tracks and three icons derived from an open-source icon set; because countless apps ship those files unmodified, our binary shared exact content with unrelated apps.

Submitting the same app under different names or accounts, and store text or screenshots that closely mirror other apps, also trigger 4.3.

1) Don't resubmit straight away — reply first

Repeated 4.3 rejections stay on your account's record. In App Store Connect, replying from the rejected submission's Messages section ("Reply to App Review") does not use up a new review.

Say briefly who you are, that you built the app yourself and did not use a template or purchased code, and ask which app you were matched with. If they answer, you can fix the exact cause; if not, send the corrected build a few days later.

2) Audit your bundle for files shared with other apps

List music and sound effects, icons, fonts, sample images and anything that came from third-party kits. If you ship a widely distributed file unmodified, your binary carries the same content as everyone else who ships it.

Replace those files with original work or assets you hold a licence for, and list each change in your submission notes.

3) Document your development history

Apple does not know you; dated evidence that you built the app over time helps. For FluxCore we shared the dates of our Google Play closed-test releases — five builds between March and July 2026, then production — as a table.

Release history, repository records or release notes weaken the impression of an app spun out of a template. Avoid claims you cannot back up: an argument the dates do not support costs credibility.

4) Make the difference visible (especially for 4.3b)

Show what your app does differently on first launch and on the store page: an original mechanic, a different audience, a real use case.

Move your description and screenshots away from the patterns other apps use. In a saturated category, meet the "meaningfully different or improved experience" bar with concrete features.

5) Resubmit with an explanatory note, appeal if needed

Send the new build with the changes written in the App Review notes. If it is rejected again you can file a reasoned appeal through Apple's App Review Board appeal form.

Keep a record of what you sent at each step; resending the same argument unchanged does not change the outcome.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a 4.3 rejection mean my account will be closed?

One rejection does not close an account. Apple does state that repeated submissions of low-quality or copycat apps may lead to removal from the Developer Program, so do not resubmit the same build without fixing it.

What is the difference between 4.3(a) and 4.3(b)?

4.3(a) covers the same app under several Bundle IDs and apps that closely resemble other apps; 4.3(b) covers apps that add nothing new in a saturated category.

Does publishing on Android first help?

Not on its own. A dated development history is supporting evidence, but the rejection stays until shared binary content or indistinguishability is fixed.

Does replying affect my place in the review queue?

A reply on a rejected submission does not start a new build review; the team can answer you in writing.
