Google Play Closed Testing: The 12 Testers, 14 Days Rule and Production Access

If your personal Google Play developer account was created after 13 November 2023, you cannot publish straight to production; you have to complete a closed test first. This guide covers the rule, the steps and what we learned shipping our own apps.

11 November 2026Google PlayAndroidClosed testing

Quick Answer

Personal developer accounts created after 13 November 2023 must run a closed test with at least 12 testers who stay opted in for at least 14 consecutive days. Once that is met, you apply for production access from "Apply for production" on the Play Console Dashboard; Google typically reviews applications within 7 days. A tester who opts out and rejoins starts their 14 days again.

Who does the rule apply to?

According to Play Console Help, the requirement applies to personal developer accounts created after 13 November 2023. Organization accounts are not covered by it.

Source: App testing requirements for new personal developer accounts.

What exactly is required?

A closed testing track with at least 12 testers who stay opted in for at least 14 consecutive days. A tester who leaves and rejoins resets their count; the 14 days must be consecutive.

Once met, "Apply for production" opens on the Play Console Dashboard. The form has three sections: your closed test, your app and your production readiness. It asks how you recruited testers, the feedback you received, your audience, expected first-year installs and what you changed after testing.

Closed testing step by step

1. Create the app in Play Console and complete the required sections such as the store listing and content rating.

2. Open a track under Test → Closed testing and upload your AAB.

3. Add testers by email list or a Google Group; a group makes adding and removing people easier.

4. Share the opt-in link. Testers must opt in through the link and install the app from the Play Store; being on the list is not enough.

5. Keep an eye on who stays opted in over the 14 days and collect feedback.

Tips for getting production access

Start with 15–20 people, not 12. Someone leaving resets their count; a buffer reduces the risk of restarting the 14 days.

Ship builds during the test. In FluxCore's Google Play closed test we released five builds between March and July 2026; changes made from feedback can then be described concretely in the form.

Keep a record of feedback. Answer "what did you change after testing" with specific changes, not general sentences.

Google may ask you to keep testing if fewer than 12 testers remain opted in or tester engagement is too low.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can my 12 testers be family and friends?

Google does not restrict who they are; what matters is that they stay opted in for 14 days and actually use the app. The form asks how you recruited them.

What happens if a tester leaves?

Their 14 days reset; if they rejoin, their count starts again. Starting with more than 12 people is the safe option.

How long does the application take?

Google says it typically reviews applications within 7 days, though it can take longer.

Does an organization account avoid the rule?

The rule is for personal accounts. An organization account needs extra verification, such as a D-U-N-S number.
